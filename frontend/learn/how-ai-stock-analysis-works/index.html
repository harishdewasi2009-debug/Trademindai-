How AI Stock Analysis Works (and Where It Fails)

By TradeMind AI Team · Published 2026-10-11 · Updated 2026-10-11

The short version

AI stock analysis is usually two steps. First, software computes numbers from market data: moving averages, RSI, volatility. Second, a language model turns those numbers into a readable summary. The model is not watching the market; it is describing a snapshot it was given.

Step 1: data and indicators

Candles (open, high, low, close, volume) are fetched for a chosen timeframe. Formulas then produce indicators. These steps are deterministic: the same candles always give the same RSI.

Step 2: interpretation

The model receives the indicator values and writes about trend, momentum and risk. Different models may phrase or weigh things differently, which is why some platforms show several models side by side.

Where it fails

  • Stale or missing data produces confident-sounding but wrong text.
  • Models can contradict themselves between runs.
  • Markets react to news that no indicator contains.
  • A clean explanation is not a forecast.

How to use it sensibly

Treat the output as a structured checklist for your own research. Verify the numbers on a chart, size positions for the possibility that you are wrong, and remember that no tool, AI or otherwise, can promise returns.
